"What do you think about [UBI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universal_basic_income)?" or: "How about those electric cars, eh? Do you think they're really environmentally-friendly?" or finally: "What do you think about green energy?" are all excellent "dinner conversation topics," DCT for friends. Many well-educated middle-class people have some thoughts on these matters, and they are easy and somehow exciting to hypothesize about (especially the UBI thing). Most people don't really have a well-formed opinion on matters of physics, genetics or mathematics, and even about liberal arts topics like architecture, a surprisingly big portion of my friends at least doesn't have an opinion. Perhaps I'm hanging with the wrong crowd? It could very well be, but I've another theory.

There are certain topics that we all, willing to smaller or greater extent, consider ourselves experts in.

"Epistemic Trespassing" is a philosophical term coined after an influential paper written in 2018 by [Nathan Ballantyne](https://faculty.fordham.edu/nballantyne/) and [published in Mind](https://doi.org/10.1093/mind/fzx042).
