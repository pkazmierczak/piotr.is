<a href="http://www.stephenledrew.com/">Stephen LeDrew</a> wrote <a href="http://blog.oup.com/2015/11/legacy-new-atheism/">an interesting post</a> about the influence the so-called "New Atheism" movement had on society, pointing out some intriguing similarities between our militant atheists and, surprisingly, the far right wing conservatism. The one observation which I don't find completely accurate, and I think it's because I live in Europe, is that the "New Atheism" isn't regarded highly in well-educated circles any more. I found a surprising number of people working in philosophy, logic, computer science and especially in natural sciences to still cherish Dawkins <em>et al.</em>, which was always rather surprising to me. It most likely has to do with a rather loose coupling between "New Atheists" and any political movements in Europe (modulo UK perhaps?).[^1]

In any case, the article is worth a read, and <a href="https://global.oup.com/academic/product/the-evolution-of-atheism-9780190225179">LeDrew's book</a> lands on my wishlist.

[^1]: <a href="http://karolinakrzyzanowska.com">K</a> made a good comment here by pointing out another difference between aggressive atheists in Europe and in the US: here they don't have an aggressive counterpart. Religious fundamentalism especially in its protestant form is extremely rare in Europe, and there isn't much political debate in which Christian fundamentalists would be visible. This may also explain why "New Atheists" don't see themselves as *avantgarde* on the old continent.
