Despite what you might hear me saying, I like TV shows. I don’t own a TV set (in fact I haven’t lived in an apartment with one for many years), and I avoid mentioning my interest in some shows, but that’s mostly due to my somewhat snobbish nature – I would like to be seen as a person who doesn’t fall for easy entertainment, and isn’t interested in anything less than a Booker prize winning novel, an inaccessible contemporary jazz album, or modern art exhibition. But in the privacy of my own blog I am willing to admit that there are certain things on television that I really enjoy, and one<a id="ref1" href="#1"><sup>1</sup></a> of these things – AMC’s Breaking Bad – just <em>came to an end</em>.

Breaking Bad is a simple show. It’s not very sophisticated, like Mad Men. It isn’t as spectacular as Game of Thrones, and not as intellectual as Girls. The story is in fact very similar to <a href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weeds_(TV_series)">Weeds</a> (at least in the beginning), but in contrast to the light-toned Showtime series, Breaking Bad is seldom funny.<a id="ref2" href="#2"><sup>2</sup></a> It is fantastically written, it has great characters, awesome cinematography, and, above all, amazing actors. But all this doesn’t explain the Breaking Bad phenomenon to me, i.e. I can’t understand <em>how</em> is it possible that a relatively simple TV series like that gets <em>so much</em> media coverage.

It’s not just the sheer amount<a id="ref3" href="#3"><sup>3</sup></a> of words that have been written about the show that amazes me, but also <em>where</em> the pieces were published. That BoingBoing <a href="http://boingboing.net/author/kmmcfarland">regularly publishes</a> posts about Breaking Bad isn’t that surprising (although these are relatively long-form essays, featured columns if you may, unlike most short notes that appear on BoingBoing), but if The New Yorker writes about shows other than Girls and Mad Men, it’s a serious matter. And it’s not only the <a href="http://www.newyorker.com/online/blogs/culture/">Culture Desk</a>, oh no. There are <a href="http://www.newyorker.com/arts/critics/television/2012/08/27/120827crte_television_nussbaum">longer pieces</a> in the magazine itself, as well as a recently published <a href="http://www.newyorker.com/reporting/2013/09/16/130916fa_fact_friend">profile</a> of Bryan Cranston. That <a href="http://tv.nytimes.com/2013/10/01/arts/television/breaking-bad-finale.html">The New York Times</a>, <a href="http://www.theguardian.com/tv-and-radio/tvandradioblog/2013/sep/30/vince-gilligan-breaking-bad-finale-felina">The Guardian</a> and even <a href="http://www.faz.net/aktuell/feuilleton/medien/das-finale-von-breaking-bad-ich-war-richtig-lebendig-12597740.html">FAZ</a> have published articles about the show doesn’t surprise me so much, but once I stumbled upon Breaking Bad in <a href="http://www.economist.com/news/business/21586801-best-show-television-also-first-rate-primer-business-breaking-bad-school">The Economist</a>, I realized there’s no where to run, no where to hide.
